## Networking Playbook

**Core strategy:**
1. Wide net of alumni — mid-level product managers (PMs) and operators are often more helpful than Vice Presidents (VPs)
2. Goal of the call: learn about the role and company; have them pass your resume to the hiring manager
3. Connect on something real — the introduction that sticks is specific, not generic

**3-bullet outreach format:**
- Who you are / what you're focused on at Kellogg
- Why specifically them / why now (one researched detail)
- One clear ask or question

**Questions that actually work:**
- "What would make you *not* recommend this role to a friend?"
- "What do people consistently get wrong about what this team actually does?"
- "What does a strong first 90 days look like here?"

**Always close with:** "Is there one other person you think I should talk to?"

**Play the student card.** This window closes at graduation. Use it generously now.

## Negotiation Quick Ref

Before any negotiation, prep:
- **Their profile** — what do they need? What pressures are they under?
- **My profile** — interests, priorities, non-negotiables
- **My ambition** — what does great look like, not just acceptable?
- **My differentiators** — why am I worth negotiating with?

Differentiation is leverage. The clearer you can articulate what you bring, the better your position.

### Story: Roca Water — Deck vs. Reality
**Type:** Analytical conviction / Due diligence
**The situation:** Led impact and management due diligence on Roca Water, an early-stage electrochemical wastewater startup, for SAN Ventures Investor Committee presentation.
**The tension:** The deck cited a $45B+ total addressable market (TAM). When we interviewed the founder (Ethan), he described the realistic near-term opportunity as $2.5–3B.
**What I did:** Cross-referenced every major claim in the deck against the founder interview and independent sources (EPA, IPCC). Flagged the $8B aquaculture losses and $10B wasted ammonia as unverified marketing figures. Documented the TAM discrepancy explicitly in the impact analysis report.
**The result:** The IC presentation led with our verified findings, not the deck narrative. The committee had an accurate picture of realistic opportunity vs. aspirational framing.
**What it taught me:** Founder decks are marketing documents. The job of diligence is to find where the story breaks. The most important skill isn't knowing what to look for — it's being willing to say out loud that the numbers don't add up.
**When to use it:** "Tell me about a time you pushed back on a narrative" · "Analytical rigor" · "Impact investing" · "Working with ambiguous or incomplete information"

### Story: Mars Candy / AI Storytelling (Activation Story — SYAYI)
**Type:** Insight / Craft
**The situation:** In Building Intelligent Products, a professor described working with Mars candy on a market share problem in India. The CEO had paid $11M for a McKinsey report that said: "get people to eat more candy." A small team built an AI model instead. When asked the same question, the AI recommended spending 80% of the marketing budget on a campaign targeting 45–55 year olds with the slogan "We made it" — and wove a story about how children born in the 1970s in India first experienced M&Ms as a rare treat brought from the US by relatives.
**The tension:** The AI's recommendation was essentially the same as McKinsey's — but framed as a story with a point of view (POV). The CEO was sold.
**What I did:** Used this as an activation story for SYAYI to illustrate that even AI output with uncertain veracity (hallucinated or not?) sold the CEO because it was *structured as a story with a POV*.
**What it taught me:** The story is the vehicle. A recommendation without a narrative is just data. The McKinsey deck had 196 slides; the AI had one story. The story won.
**When to use it:** Pitches or workshops about AI and communication · "Why does storytelling matter in business?" · Any context where the power of narrative framing is the point

## SYAYI — Selling Yourself and Your Ideas
*Course: Spring 2026 | Key frameworks for personal communication, pitching, and presence*

### Course Modules
1. **Story** — Every effective idea starts with a narrative. The structure: context → tension → resolution. A good story is specific, has a real protagonist, and creates a moment of insight.
2. **Brand** — Your personal brand is what people say about you when you're not in the room. The Brand Reflection Matrix: identify your adjectives, test against others' perceptions, resolve the gap.
3. **Pitch** — The 4 Box framework: Problem · Solution · Why You · Why Now. A pitch earns the next meeting, not a final yes.
4. **Point of View (POV)** — A POV is a defensible position, not a description. "I believe [X] because [Y]." Executives and investors trust people who have one.
5. **Prototypes** — Pitch the prototype, not the idea. A physical or visual artifact forces specificity and reveals assumptions.
6. **Presenting** — Slides support you, they don't replace you. Lead with the conclusion. Never read the slide.

### Key Assignments
- **Personal Brand adjectives** — Identified core descriptors; tested for alignment vs. how others perceive you
- **Origin Story** — Written narrative of how you got here; the through-line of background → Kellogg → future
- **Activation Story** — A vivid, specific anecdote that illustrates your POV in action (see Mars story above)
- **4 Box** — Mapped Problem/Solution/Why You/Why Now for your personal pitch
- **Breakthrough Email** — Cold outreach exercise: how to get a stranger to respond in one email
- **UTalk Draft** — Short personal presentation (3–5 min) on a topic you care about
- **Shashank Intro (PPTX)** — Personal introduction slide deck used for the UTalk format
- **Reflection 1 & "Owning Every Room"** — Written reflections on presence, confidence, and showing up fully

### The MAP & GPS Framework (from Whole Brain Communication — Fall 2025)
- **MAP** (Message-Audience-Purpose): What's the one thing you want them to remember? Who is your audience? What do you want them to do?
- **GPS** (Goal-Path-Signal): What's your goal? What's the path to get there? What's the signal that you've arrived?

Use MAP & GPS to design any communication — email, slide, presentation, or pitch — before you start building.
