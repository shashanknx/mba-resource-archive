## Frameworks

### The 3Cs — Customer · Company · Competitor
The starting lens for any marketing strategy. Find where your **company's** unique strengths overlap with **customer** needs while staying distinct from **competitors**.

- **Customer** — what job are they hiring you for? What do they need?
- **Company** — what are our distinctive strengths and strategic resources?
- **Competitor** — what are others offering, and where can we be different?

**Avoid the middle.** Where all three overlap with low differentiation, you get price wars and mediocrity ("freaky margin hell"). Marketing is the strategic process of creating, capturing, and delivering value — far broader than advertising. Shift from product-centric to **customer-centric**: identify the specific "job" the customer hires the product to do.

---

### STP — Segmentation · Targeting · Positioning

**Segmentation:** Divide the market into groups with meaningfully different needs.
Bases: Demographic · Geographic · Psychographic · Behavioral (usage, loyalty, benefits sought)
Good segments are: measurable, substantial, accessible, differentiable, actionable.

**Targeting:** Choose which segment(s) to serve. Evaluate on: size, growth rate, competitive intensity, fit with your capabilities.

**Positioning:** How you want to be perceived in your target segment's mind.
> *"For [segment], [brand] is the [frame of reference] that [POD — point of difference], because [RTB — reason to believe]."*

*"A product for everyone rarely reaches anyone."* The goal is to be the obvious choice for a specific segment, not the adequate choice for everyone.

### Paths to Growth
- **Penetration** — steal share from competitors (differentiation or price)
- **New to category** — bring in non-users entirely (e.g., Uber vs. car ownership)
- **Usage/Upsell** — increase how much existing customers buy
- **New segments** — expand to adjacent demographics, geographies, or use cases

### Pricing Strategy

**Rational Buyer Framework:**
- **Reservation Price (RP)** = max willingness to pay
- **Surplus** = RP − Price; buyers maximize surplus
- Customer buys from you when: Surplus(you) ≥ Surplus(alternative)

**EVC — Economic Value to Customer:**
*EVC = Price of reference product + Incremental benefit of your product*
Sets your pricing ceiling. Focus only on the incremental value over the next best alternative.

**The margin rule:** Pricing is a gamble between margin and volume. A 1% price increase beats a 1% cost decrease because volume increases also increase variable costs. Price is the highest-leverage lever in the profit-and-loss statement (P&L).

**Price discrimination:**
- 1st degree — charge each customer their exact RP (theoretical)
- 2nd degree — versioning/quantity (tiered plans, volume discounts)
- 3rd degree — segment-based (student discounts, geography)

**Key rule:** To capture value, align *what you sell* with *what creates the value*. Pricing by dose only makes sense if buyers know dosage in advance.

### Strategic Resources — The Foundation of Differentiation
Differentiation is the foundation of pricing power. Without it, the market dictates price. Differentiation must rest on **strategic resources** — assets that pass four tests:

1. **Satisfy customers** — the resource creates value customers care about
2. **Differentiate** — it makes you distinct from competitors
3. **Sustainable** — it holds up over time (3–5 years), hard to imitate
4. **Owned & controlled** — the firm owns it, not a partner or supplier

**Brand** is a critical strategic resource — especially when built on *non-searchable* attributes (trust, service, reliability) that are harder to copy than technical specs. If you lose a technological edge, pivot to new niches (service, customization) to keep an advantage.

### Loyalty, Acquisition & Retention
Acquisition and retention need *different* strategies — acquisition attracts new users; retention runs a funnel of evolving benefits to grow customer lifetime value (CLV).

- **Loyalty programs** must drive *incremental* behavior — purchases that wouldn't have happened otherwise — not reward what customers were already doing.
- **Propensity vs. uplift models:** A propensity model predicts *who is likely to buy*; an uplift model predicts *who can be persuaded to buy*. Target the persuadable to avoid wasting spend on sure things and lost causes.
- **New product development** is risky (~95% fail). Generate many ideas, screen with a **stage-gate system**, and validate with **behavioral feedback** (pre-orders, deposits) over verbal affirmation. Small incremental changes (e.g., a cup holder) can drive outsized satisfaction.

---

### Channel Strategy & Repositioning
Channel relationships live in the tension between **collaboration** (creating value together) and **bargaining** (deciding who captures it).

- Bargaining power sits with whoever **"owns" the customer**. Increase leverage by raising your own differentiation or undermining a partner's.
- Beware over-reliance on a single distributor (e.g., Amazon) — it erodes margin and control over time.
- **Repositioning — "fix then promise":** make the operational improvements *before* making new marketing promises. Repositioning is costly; don't alienate the core customer while chasing aspirational ones.

### Marketing Math
- **Margin** = (Price − Cost) / Price (% of retail)
- **Markup** = (Price − Wholesale) / Wholesale (% of wholesale)
- **Unit contribution** = Revenue/unit − Variable cost/unit
- **Break-even** = Fixed Costs / Unit Contribution
- **Profit** = (Units × Unit Contribution) − Fixed Costs

**Retailer dynamics:** Dropping your price lowers the dollar margin retailers earn → less incentive for them to push your product. % margin vs. unit margin can move in opposite directions.

### Research Design

**Types of data:**
- **Generated data** (reviews, usage logs) — high volume; watch for selection bias
- **Experiments** (A/B tests) — reveals causal effect on behavior
- **Surveys** — stated preference; people underreport sensitive behaviors

**Backward research design:**
1. What decision does the manager need to make?
2. What does the final report need to show?
3. What analyses populate that report?
4. What data is needed?

Start with the decision. Design backward. Surprising conclusions should be anticipated, not discovered.

**Survivorship bias:** You only see the planes that came back. The most diagnostic data is often what's missing. Always ask: what am I not seeing, and why?

**Conjoint analysis:** Present hypothetical product bundles varying on attributes. Model which attributes drive choice and the dollar value of each level (part-worth utilities). Use for: pricing decisions, feature prioritization, new product design.

### Quant Methods in Marketing
- **Regression** — what drives an outcome variable? Quantifies how each input moves the outcome (see also experimentation and control limits in [7_operations](7_operations.md)).
- **Factor analysis** — reduce survey variables to latent dimensions (what customers actually care about)
- **Cluster analysis** — group customers into natural segments without predefined categories

**Tools (Marketing Research & Analytics course — Winter 2026):** R + Radiant (browser-based R GUI). Key quizzes: Radiant basics (Quiz 2), one- and two-sample means comparison (Quiz 3), regression analysis (Quiz 4), factor analysis (Quiz 5), cluster analysis (Quiz 6). In-class datasets: BlueApron (retention), Cars (conjoint), Movie (experimental design).

**Pricing Strategies quant tools (MECN 446 — Spring 2026):** Excel-based models for EVC, price discrimination, willingness-to-pay (WTP) estimation, reserve price optimization, and bundling. Homeworks 1–7 covered: break-even, elasticity, price discrimination design, bundling, auction/reserve pricing, and case applications.

**Backward research design (MRA applied):**
A well-designed research project for a real client (SHFT, a mental health startup) required:
1. Client intake: understand their decisions, target segments, and gaps
2. Survey design in Qualtrics: screener, attitudinal and behavioral scales, demographics
3. Data cleaning: handling missing values, recoding, codebook documentation
4. Analysis: factor analysis to surface latent dimensions → cluster analysis to identify segments → regression to find what drives the key outcome
5. Deliverable: recommendation memo + formal knitted PDF report (R Markdown)

## Mental Models

**Value creation ≠ value capture.** Creating value doesn't mean you'll keep it. Appropriability (intellectual property, switching costs, complementary assets) determines how much you actually capture. See 1_design_innovation for Teece framework.

**"People don't want to feel like the sense of fairness is broken."** Even optimal price discrimination creates backlash when customers discover it. Price strategically; watch for trust erosion.
