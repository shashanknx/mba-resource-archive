### Roca Water — Live Impact Investing Due Diligence
**Context:** Full due diligence case within SSIM-940 (Spring 2026). Roca Water is an early-stage electrochemical water treatment startup focused on recovering ammonium from wastewater sidestreams and converting it into fertilizer.

**The problem Roca solves:** Nitrogen pollution (eutrophication of coastal/river ecosystems; N₂O emissions from conventional treatment) + the fertilizer supply chain relies on the Haber-Bosch process, which is energy-intensive. Roca's electrochemical system recovers ammonium that would otherwise be wasted, producing fertilizer as a byproduct.

**Company stage:** Prototype only. Two LOIs signed with Bay Area municipal wastewater plants for future pilots. No deployed systems. First on-site trials targeted 2027; commercial deployment 2028–2029. No commercial procurement track record.

**What I/team produced:**
- **Impact Analysis** — Verified founder claims against EPA/IPCC sources. Confirmed ~50% of ammonia lost to wastewater annually; eutrophication widespread; N₂O more potent than CO₂. Flagged: $8B aquaculture losses and $10B wasted ammonia cited in deck were *unverified* marketing figures; first pilot data needed. TAM framing in deck ($45B+) significantly overstates Ethan's (founder's) own characterization (~$2.5–3B for U.S. ammonium recovery; ~$20B global including agriculture). Go-to-market: U.S. only, not Europe as deck implied.
- **Management Assessment** — Evaluated founding team capability, commercial execution risk (zero GTM track record beyond sampling), and ability to convert LOIs into paid pilots
- **Financial Returns Analysis** — Built returns model; analyzed path from pilots to scale revenue
- **Due Diligence Questions** — Final draft sent to founder (May 3, 2026); circled back on unverified figures and TAM discrepancies
- **Investor Committee Presentation** — Full investment memo (v.2) + deck presented to committee (May 25, 2026)

**Key diligence findings:**
- Technology performance claims (selectivity >20, >4,000 hours durability, <3 kWh/kg-N) unverified — need first pilot data
- Impact logic model is directionally credible but execution-dependent
- Deck overstates TAM vs. what founder actually described; treat deck figures as marketing until substantiated
- Two LOIs ≠ revenue; the critical unknown is whether the team can convert them into paid contracts

**What this demonstrates:** Full due diligence lifecycle (impact analysis, management assessment, financial modeling, IC presentation); rigorous skepticism of founder claims; ability to reconcile deck vs. interview vs. third-party data.

**Stories to pull:** "The deck said $45B TAM; the founder described $2.5–3B." How critical diligence revealed the gap between marketing narrative and realistic opportunity — and what to do with that.

### Department Scoping Tracker — BIP Final Project
**Course:** Building Intelligent Products (Spring 2026)

**What it is:** A single-file Flask + SQLite local web app for running department technology-scoping engagements end to end. Built as the capstone for the Building Intelligent Products course. Deployed from a master prompt (Claude/Codex-generated) into a working prototype.

**The problem it solves:** IT stakeholder alignment is broken. Engineers, budget owners, functional leads, and downstream teams have misaligned requirements → poorly adopted software → wasted spend. The tracker gives a project lead a structured workflow: intake a request, interview stakeholders, draft requirements, scope the project, and track phase progress in one place.

**Tech stack:** Python / Flask / SQLite (no external dependencies beyond Flask). Single file: `app.py`. Run with `pip install -r requirements.txt && python app.py`.

**Data model:** Requests (title, requestor, problem, urgency, financial impact, phase) → Stakeholders (name, role, influence, interview status) → Requirements (text, priority, stakeholder attribution).

**How it was built:** A master prompt was written that instructed Claude/Codex to build the entire app in one unattended pass. The prompt specified the tech stack, data model, UI requirements, and coding standards — demonstrating prompt engineering as a product development skill.

**What this demonstrates:** Applied AI product development, prompt engineering, full-stack prototyping (even with simple tools), translating a product insight into working software.

**Stories to pull:** "I wrote a prompt, not code — and the output was a running app." The meta-insight about what changes when AI can generate working software from a well-scoped prompt.

### EyeMax — Warby Parker Smart Glasses Concept
**What it is:** An augmented-eyewear concept (Research Design Build / Growth Innovation) for Warby Parker — a single, all-day pair of smart glasses combining adaptive, all-distance lenses with real-time eye-health diagnostics, so users stop switching between regular, reading, and sun glasses. Framed around a hypothetical $150M Google partnership on the Android XR (extended reality) platform.

**My role:** Concept lead / product strategist. Defined the growth hypothesis, brand principles, feature set, and testing plan.

**Guiding principles (brand mandates):**
- **Invisible First, Intelligent Second** — tech disappears into a normal frame; avoid the "dorky"/"social pariah" stigma of Meta Ray-Bans.
- **Extend the Brand to Extend the Self** — frames must look/feel like designer acetate to preserve self-expression.
- **Earn the Right to be Worn All Day** — comfort and durability above all.

**Core features:** Smart Zoom / vision enhancement (subtle heads-up display, or HUD, to magnify distant text); environment-responsive tint (instant, vs. laggy transition lenses); eye-care & health diagnostics (ultraviolet, or UV, exposure and eye strain).

**Riskiest assumptions tested (7 Elements scorecard):** the "one pair" behavior change (users keep backup glasses); durability & 16+ hr battery; fitting battery/processing into a slim profile without the "creep factor"; need for in-store "simulation rooms" given premium pricing.

**Business impact:** Enables premium lens pricing, higher customer lifetime value (CLV) via ecosystem loyalty, and fewer prescription replacements.

**What this demonstrates:** Product strategy, design research, assumption-driven testing, hardware/software realism (see also AI frontier notes in [10_ai_technology](10_ai_technology.md)).
