# Design & Innovation
*Courses: Appropriability by Design (Summer) · Innovation Strategy (Winter) · Research Design Build (Fall) · Growth Innovation (Spring)*

> Use this when building or evaluating a new product, understanding users, or thinking about how to protect and capture value from an innovation.

## Quick Reference

- **DVF (Desirability · Viability · Feasibility)** — Does anyone want it? Can you make money? Can you build it?
- **JTBD (Jobs-To-Be-Done)** — People don't buy products, they hire them for a job. Find the job.
- **HMW (How Might We)** — Reframe the problem before jumping to solutions.
- **AEIOU (Activities · Environments · Interactions · Objects · Users)** — A fieldwork lens for observing users in context.
- **Business Model Canvas** — 9-block map of how a business creates, delivers, and captures value.
- **MSCW (MoSCoW)** — prioritize requirements: Must / Should / Could / Won't have.
- **Teece** — Two variables determine who profits from an innovation: IP strength and complementary assets.

## Frameworks

### DVF — Desirability · Viability · Feasibility
The sweet spot is the intersection of all three.
- **Desirability** — Do real customers want this? Will they pay?
- **Viability** — Does the business model work?
- **Feasibility** — Can you actually build and deliver it?

A desirable + feasible idea with no viable model is a charity. A viable + feasible idea nobody wants is a solution in search of a problem.

---

### JTBD — Jobs-To-Be-Done
People hire products to do a job — functional, emotional, or social. Understand the job, not the demographic.

**Warby Parker:** The job wasn't "buy glasses." It was "feel smart and stylish without paying a premium tax." They competed against an industry assumption, not another eyewear brand.

**JTBD interview questions:**
- What were you doing right before you looked for this solution?
- What have you tried before? What frustrated you?
- What would you do if this didn't exist?
- What does success look like in 6 months?

---

### HMW — How Might We
Reframe problems before generating solutions. Format: *"How might we [verb] [subject] so that [outcome]?"*

Too narrow = constrains solutions. Too broad = no direction. Find the level of abstraction that opens creative space without losing focus.

**Step / Stretch / Leap:** Generate concepts at three levels — incremental improvement, analogous solution from another domain, radical reimagining.

---

### AEIOU — Field Research
Five lenses for observing users in their actual context:
- **A**ctivities — what are they doing and trying to accomplish?
- **E**nvironments — where are they? What's the setting?
- **I**nteractions — how do they interact with objects, systems, people?
- **O**bjects — what tools and artifacts are they using?
- **U**sers — who are they? What are their roles and values?

Observation → Patterns → Insights → HMWs. An observation is "users refresh the page repeatedly." An insight is "users don't trust the system's status indicators, so they create their own."

### Appropriability — Profiting from Innovation (Teece)
Two variables determine whether you capture the value you create:
1. **Appropriability regime** — How strong is intellectual property (IP) protection? How easy is imitation?
2. **Complementary assets** — Do you need to control manufacturing, distribution, or brand to commercialize?

**IP types at a glance:**
- **Patents** — must be new, useful, non-obvious, enabled. Expensive. File a Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) application for international protection.
- **Trade secrets** — no disclosure required; last forever if protected. Best for processes.
- **Trademarks** — source and expression layer; deeper moat than patents once established.
- **Copyright** — original works in any tangible medium.

**The Sandwich:** Innovators get squeezed between IP holders above and manufacturers below. Escape by owning the IP *and* complementary assets, or by commoditizing one layer to capture value in another.

### MSCW — MoSCoW Prioritization
*Use when:* Scoping a product, minimum viable product (MVP), or release — deciding what makes the cut when you can't build everything. Sort every requirement/feature into one of four buckets:

- **M — Must have** — non-negotiable; the product fails or is unusable without it.
- **S — Should have** — important and high-value, but not vital; there's a workaround if it slips.
- **C — Could have** — nice-to-have; include only if time and resources allow. First to be cut.
- **W — Won't have (this time)** — explicitly out of scope for now; parked, not forgotten.

Forces hard trade-off conversations and prevents scope creep. The discipline is naming the **Won'ts** out loud — and keeping the **Musts** a genuine minority. Pairs with the stage-gate system for deciding *what* to build inside an approved concept.

### Innovation Strategy — Hedgehog Concept
*From Jim Collins / Winter Innovation Strategy (Course: Winter 2026)*

The **Hedgehog Concept** sits at the intersection of three circles:
1. **What you're deeply passionate about** — What creates genuine energy and motivation?
2. **What you can be the best in the world at** — Not what you *want* to be best at, but what you *can* be.
3. **What drives your economic engine** — What's the key metric? (Revenue per customer, per geography, per product type)

Great companies (and people) don't spread across all three — they find the one thing at the intersection and pursue it with discipline.

**Applied to career/product:** Use when evaluating a role, a venture idea, or a company's strategy. If a company is pursuing something outside the intersection of these three, it's unlikely to win long-term.

### Innovation Strategy — InSight / Offering / Testing Frameworks
*Templates from Winter Innovation Strategy course*

**InSight Framework:** Synthesize field research into an actionable innovation direction.
- **Observation** → what you saw/heard in the field
- **Tension** → the unmet need or frustration this reveals
- **Opportunity** → the "how might we" that follows

**Offering Framework:** Translate insight into a testable concept.
- Define the offering: what is it, for whom, doing what job?
- Define the "wow moment" — the moment the user first understands the value
- Identify the riskiest assumption: what has to be true for this to work?

**Testing & Prioritization:** Not all assumptions are equal. Prioritize by:
- **Impact if wrong** — how catastrophic is it?
- **Uncertainty** — how much do you actually know?
- Test the riskiest, most uncertain assumptions first

**Top-Down Sizing:** Size a market from macro (total population) → addressable (who has the problem) → obtainable (who you can reach/convert). The discipline: state your assumptions explicitly so they can be challenged.

**Research "Aha Moment" Template:** Document insights from field research in structured form — Observation → Pattern → Insight → Implication. Avoid writing observations as interpretations; keep them factual until the insight step.

### Innovation Postures (3M Framework)
- **Core (70%)** — sustaining improvements to existing products and markets
- **Adjacent (20%)** — new uses for existing capabilities, nearby markets
- **Transformational (10%)** — new category creation, disruption

Don't starve the core. Don't neglect transformation and get disrupted.

### Growth Innovation — Pre-Mortem
*Assignment: Spring 2026 — applied to EyeMax and Disney innovation exercises*

Before launching: "Imagine it's 12 months from now and the project has failed. What happened?"

Forces the team to articulate risks they're holding privately. Better than a post-mortem because you can still do something about it.

**How to run one:** Set the scene (it failed; be vivid). Ask each person to write 3–5 reasons individually. Share and cluster. Turn the highest-risk items into explicit mitigation plans or kill criteria.

---

### Liquid Expectations
Customers compare their experience with you to the best experience they've had *anywhere* — not just in your category. Netflix sets hospitality expectations. Amazon sets B2B purchasing expectations.

Three competitor types: **Direct** (same category), **Experiential** (replaces the need for you), **Perceptual** (shapes what customers expect).

## Mental Models

**Invention ≠ Innovation.** Innovation = creation *and* capture of new value. You can invent something and have someone else profit from it entirely.

**The job is the unit of analysis.** Not the product, not the persona — the job the customer is trying to get done. Everything follows from understanding that.
